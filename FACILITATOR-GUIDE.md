# Facilitator Guide — Running a 20–30 Minute Session

This covers all three modules: the Fake Wifi Portal demo, the 7-question
Spot the Phish quiz, and the 6-ticket Blue Team Challenge. It's written to
keep a room of 16–18 year-olds on task without feeling like a lecture.

## Before the session

- Load the site on the room's screen/projector and on each student device
  beforehand so you're not waiting on wifi/logins when the session starts.
- Decide pairs vs. solo. For a 20–30 min slot with a class-sized group,
  **pairing students (2 per device)** keeps focus higher than solo screens —
  there's social pressure to stay on task and a built-in discussion partner.
- If devices are a distraction risk, have students keep devices **flat on
  the desk, lid at ~45°**, and only open to the site — no other tabs. Walk
  the room during activities rather than standing at the front, which keeps
  everyone visibly on-task without needing to police it directly.
- Have the debrief questions below visible on the room screen so there's
  always something to look at between activities, not an open gap.

## Timing breakdown (25-minute version — trim as needed)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Hook: ask "who's used free wifi at a mall or café?" then "would you ever type your school or email password into one?" Let a couple of answers land, then say "let's see why that question matters." |
| 2:00–7:00 | **Module 1 — Fake Wifi Portal.** Project it; have one volunteer "fill it in" live while others watch, or have pairs do it on their own devices simultaneously. Immediately discuss the reveal panel's red flags together. |
| 7:00–8:00 | Transition: "Now you'll test that instinct for real — 7 rounds, see how many you catch." |
| 8:00–16:00 | **Module 2 — Spot the Phish** (pairs, own pace). ~1 min/question is plenty; most pairs finish in 6–8 minutes. |
| 16:00–17:00 | Quick show of hands: "who got 7/7? 5 or 6? fewer?" Normalize mistakes — that's the point of practicing. |
| 17:00–18:00 | Transition: "Last one flips the script — you're not the target anymore, you're the analyst stopping the attack." |
| 18:00–24:00 | **Module 3 — Blue Team Challenge** (pairs, own pace). The in-page timer is cosmetic and creates urgency without actually cutting anyone off. |
| 24:00–30:00 | **Debrief** (below) — this is the part that makes it stick, don't cut it short if you're tight on time; trim Module 3 to 5 tickets instead if needed. |

## Keeping it from feeling distracted / off-task

- **Chunk it, don't leave open time.** Each module has a clear start/stop;
  announce transitions out loud ("close that tab, next one's up") rather
  than letting students drift into finished-early scrolling.
- **Give finishers a job.** If a pair finishes Module 2 or 3 early, have
  them write down (on paper or in a shared doc) the one scenario they found
  trickiest, to share in debrief — keeps hands occupied instead of idle.
- **Walk, don't watch from the front.** Circulating keeps engagement up
  without needing to raise your voice over side conversations.
- **Use the score/badge as a hook, not a competition.** Mentioning "see if
  you can get a Gold Shield" works better than turning it into a ranked
  leaderboard, which can disengage students who score lower early on.

## Debrief prompts (last 5–6 minutes)

Ask these out loud, take a few answers each, don't need deep discussion:

1. "What's one thing every single trick in this session had in common?"
   (Target answer: asking for a password somewhere it doesn't belong, or
   creating urgency to stop you thinking.)
2. "Which one of the 7 phishing scenarios would actually have gotten you,
   if you saw it on your own phone tomorrow?" (Normalizes honesty — this
   isn't about being embarrassed.)
3. "In the Blue Team challenge, what was the 'correct but not obvious'
   answer that surprised you?"
4. Close with one rule to remember: *"A password only ever goes into the
   one real app or site it belongs to — never into a wifi screen, a pop-up,
   a DM, or a QR code."*

## If you have less than 20 minutes

Run Module 1 (5 min) + Module 2 trimmed to 4–5 questions (6–7 min) + a
60-second version of the debrief. Skip Module 3 or offer it as a take-home
link for students who want to keep going.
